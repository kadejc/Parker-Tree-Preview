Trimming Methodology

Fine Tip Pruning

Small cuts at the branch tips reduce end weight while preserving a healthy, natural canopy.

Reduced weight at the branch tips while preserving the tree’s natural shape.

Every cut matters. We favor small, precise cuts over large wounds, giving the tree a better opportunity to seal off damage and recover.

By working at the branch tips, we guide growth and reduce weight at the ends, keeping the canopy balanced and helping branches withstand storms. We keep healthy growth closer to the trunk so branches carry their weight more evenly. Stripping out that growth leaves heavy branch tips more prone to sagging and breakage.

The result is thoughtful pruning that lasts. In our experience, trees we’ve cared for throughout their lives can often go five years or more between trims, depending on their species, condition, and surroundings.

Thoughtful branch selection preserves interior foliage and encourages more uniform growth.

A healthy canopy includes leafy growth throughout the interior. We only remove anything rubbing, crossing, going in a weird direction, out of a crotch and deadwood.

We preserve healthy interior growth to encourage well-supported branches and keep weight closer to the center of the tree. Think of holding a full bucket of water at arm’s length versus resting it on your shoulder. The closer the weight is to its support, the easier it is to carry.

Keeping the interior full also encourages more even growth, helping your tree maintain its shape and reducing the need for frequent trimming.

Clearance From Structures

Create space around your home while keeping overhanging branches lighter.

Created clearance above the roof while retaining a leafy canopy.

Ideally, trees have enough space to grow naturally, away from buildings and other obstacles. In reality, they often need pruning to maintain clearance from your home, neighboring trees, streets, and other surrounding features.

We guide branch growth and reduce weight over your house, keeping overhanging branches lighter while maintaining the tree’s natural shape.

We create clearance around your structures using proper pruning practices, helping protect your home while preserving the health of your trees.

Questions about tree trimming

Reviewed by Ryder Capps, ISA Certified Arborist

What is different about your approach to trimming?

We make small, precise cuts at the branch tips and preserve healthy interior growth. Our approach combines fine tip pruning, structural pruning, and clearance around structures.

How often will my tree need trimming?

The interval depends on the species, condition, and surroundings. In our experience, some trees we have cared for throughout their lives can go five years or more between trims; that is not a fixed schedule for every tree.

When is the best time to trim trees in Texas?

Many trees can be pruned in the dormant season, from late winter before spring growth begins. Oaks are the exception in North Texas: the Texas A&M Forest Service advises avoiding oak pruning from February through June, when oak wilt spreads most easily, and painting fresh oak cuts right away. Dead, broken, or hazardous branches can be removed at any time of year. Worried about oak wilt? See tree diagnosis.

Should I trim trees near power lines myself?

No. Contact with a line can be deadly even without touching it directly, and branches can carry current. Your electric utility handles clearance around its lines; Oncor serves most of the Dallas and Plano area. We can prune the rest of the tree once the line is clear.

Do you top trees or lion-tail them?

No. Topping cuts a tree back to stubs, which leaves large wounds and weak, crowded regrowth. Lion-tailing strips out the interior and leaves foliage only at the branch ends, putting the weight exactly where the tree can least handle it. We do neither.

How should young trees be pruned?

For young trees, a light structural prune every few years sets up one strong leader and well-spaced branches. Small corrective cuts now save larger cuts later.

Can you trim branches over my house?

We guide branch growth, reduce end weight, and create clearance while preserving the tree’s natural shape. The work depends on the tree and the structures around it.

Do you work with HOAs and commercial properties?

Yes. Along with homeowners, we care for trees at HOA common areas, apartment and townhome communities, and commercial properties in Plano, Dallas, and nearby cities, including trimming, clearance, and removals. Call to talk through the property and its trees.
